How MalAvi
works after the update

This document describes the changes in the rebuild of MalAvi. It will show you how the rebuild works in sort of broad strokes. Briefly, there's a new static website with a submission button, curators review the submissions and can add papers themselves, approved submissions get queued up for the next build.

I also describe the other connected products, there's the new website of course, but also the R package malaviR, and there's a shinyapp BLAST tool (although I might replace that with something else in the near future).

Two ways data arrive

New data are incorporated in two ways. Either someone submits new data (the submission form and/or a manuscript), or a curator finds the data in a published paper and submits the data themselves. Once the data are submitted to our pipeline they will get the same checks.

The software underlying the pipeline is deterministic everywhere (not AI). There's no risk of AI hallucinated data getting into the database.

How a curator decision is made

Form is live

When a submission happens, the curators are emailed a report (with the data quality checks). At least one curator must read the report and record one of three answers ("Accept", "Flag", "Reject"). The rule that matters most is what happens when two curators disagree: an objection outranks an approval. Currently, I've put a 24 hour period after one curator approves before the submission is accepted (that time window can change...probably should be bigger). During that window, another curator can review and either accept as well or flag or reject (again the latter two choices will stop the submission going forward). Curators must discuss among each other any flagged or rejected submission before it can be resurrected and submitted again. Only one curator (currently set as V. Ellis) has the authority to override the flagged or rejected setting.

How a new version of MalAvi is generated

Being built

When the curators deem enough submissions are in, one of them will run the software that builds the new version. A diff file will be created that shows the differences from one version to another. This also allows curators to make ad hoc fixes that are identified as well.

Who does what

Curators review submitted data and can add data from papers they find, but they don't need to know any coding. Only the maintainer needs to deal with code (and the maintainer is likely also one of the curators, probably the lead curator). Note that submitters will need Google accounts to submit with the machinery I've set up, but if they can't do that, they can just email their submissions to the curators.

Submitter

Fills in and submits the template, and/or a paper. Can follow their submission's progress on the website.

A web form with Google acct, or just send an email

Curator

Reviews the report generated by submission and records a verdict.

Browser and email needed...easy.

Lead curator

Everything a curator does, plus clearing another curator's flag or overriding a rejection after discussion.

Browser and email needed...easy.

Maintainer

Keeps the software running and builds each new version.

Command line programming needed.

Unpublished data can be submitted and are held privately. This is (as it's always been) so someone can reserve a lineage name before their paper is out. The queue on the website shows the name and the date the lineage name was claimed, but does not show who claimed it or anything else about the parasite.
