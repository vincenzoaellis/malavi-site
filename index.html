Submit your data

MalAvi is built from data contributed by researchers. There are two things to send, and they can be sent months apart.

Open the submission form Check a sequence first

The form runs on Google Forms, and it asks you to sign in with a Google account because it accepts file uploads. If you do not have one, or Google is not available where you are, send the template and the PDF to malaviadmin@gmail.com instead. Email is just as welcome as the form.

1

Before you publish

Send your new lineage names and their sequences. They are checked, typically within a week, and you are told of any changes — so the same names end up in your paper, in GenBank and in MalAvi. Send the accession numbers once you have deposited.

2

When it is accepted

Send the records: which lineage, in which host, where, and how many were tested. Plus the paper and its supplementary files if you can. A curator adds them, resolves any questions with you by email, and they appear in the next release.

What to send

Submission templateOne workbook covers both stages. Fill the green columns.XLSX Worked exampleA real filled submission from Gupta et al. 2019: 47 lineages, 28 hosts, 13 sites.XLSX

The paper and its supplementary files are welcome too, and go through the same form. Neither is required. The supplementary tables usually hold the host × lineage × site matrix, so they help most. Files are used only to check the records against the paper, are shared with the curators alone, and are never posted.

Records you send go into the database — that is what submitting is for. If your study is not published yet and you would rather we held the records until it is, say so in the notes on the form and we will. Names and sequences sent before publication are held for you either way: while a submission is in the queue its proposed names are listed as claimed, so nobody else is given them.

The detail, if you want it

Stage 1: naming new lineages

A new lineage is a sequence that differs at one or more positions from the most similar sequence already in MalAvi.

  • Names are a 5–6 letter acronym from the scientific name of the first host it was found in, plus a two-digit number: Alcippe poioicephala → ALCPOI02. The name must be unique.
  • Fill the NewLineages sheet, one representative host species per lineage.
  • Fill the Reference sheet. A preliminary title is enough.
  • Give the sequences, on the Sequences sheet or as a FASTA file whose names match your proposed names.
  • Send us the accession numbers once you have deposited in GenBank.

Please send the sequences themselves whether or not they are in GenBank. Accession numbers alone are not enough.

Stage 2: the records
  • Hosts_and_Sites: one row per lineage / host species / site combination.
  • Sites: latitude and longitude as degrees and decimal minutes, e.g. 11°28.20000', 076°07.80000'. Decimal degrees are fine too; we convert and show you what we read.
  • Alt_Lineage_names: if your paper calls an existing MalAvi lineage by a different name. Please fill this in; it avoids substantial work reconciling names during curation.
  • Vectors: if you screened arthropods.

If you only have a paper

Send the PDF and its supplementary tables and tell us what they contain: host-parasite associations, localities, prevalence, vector records. We will do the extraction.

Older papers are also welcome. If you find any older papers that were missed in previous versions of MalAvi, please send them (and the supplementary materials if appropriate) to us either using the form or email.

Check a sequence

One sequence at a time. Paste it plain or with a Fasta header. This tells you whether it is already in MalAvi, which genus it looks like, and whether it is long enough to be named as new. It also runs the quality checks a curator would: how much of the barcode region you have, whether it reads through in frame, and whether any base sits where no other lineage has one. If it does look new, it will help you work out the name.

Runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.

A curator confirms every new lineage and its name.

Check a name

No sequence needed. Give the host species your new lineage came from and this shows which numbers its acronym has already used — both in the release and in submissions that are still in the queue.

About MalAvi

A database of avian malaria parasites and related haemosporidians (Plasmodium, Haemoproteus and Leucocytozoon), organized around lineages of the mitochondrial cytochrome b gene.

Avian malaria parasites Plasmodium and related haemosporidians (Haemoproteus and Leucocytozoon) multiply as haploid clones in avian hosts and undergo a sexual phase in dipteran vectors. Understanding their ecology and evolution requires detailed records of their host ranges and geographic distributions. The MalAvi database provides that service.

Please note that a molecular finding of a cytochrome b lineage by PCR is no guarantee that the species is a competent host. This requires microscopic examination of thin blood films confirming presence of gametocytes, or ultimately by controlled infection experiments.

For images of the parasites, see the P. B. Šivickis Laboratory of Parasitology in Vilnius.

Citing MalAvi

Please cite the database paper, and the release you used. Each release is versioned so your analysis stays reproducible.

Bensch S, Hellgren O, Pérez-Tris J (2009). MalAvi: a public database of malaria parasites and related haemosporidians in avian hosts based on mitochondrial cytochrome b lineages. Molecular Ecology Resources 9: 1353–1358.

Known issues in this release

The tables and the alignment are published as they are, without silent corrections, so that anyone working from a download and anyone working from the database sees the same records. Problems found in the current release are listed here; corrections are made in a later release rather than in place.
